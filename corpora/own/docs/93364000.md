# で (Japanese, particle, Wiktionary)

で (Japanese, particle, Wiktionary)

Etymology 1

The hiragana character て (te) with a dakuten (゛).

Syllable

で • (de)

The hiragana syllable で (de). Its equivalent in katakana is デ (de).

Etymology 2

Originally a contraction of にて (nite), later treated as a conjugation of the copula だ (da).

Particle

で • (de)

at, in:

indicating a location or duration at which something happens

ここで休みたい。

Koko de yasumitai.

I want to rest in here (at this location).

indicating the carriage of something

白水着で泳いでみた

Shiromizugi de oyoidemita

I tried to swim in a white swimsuit

with, by, using: indicating the means by or with which something happens

筆ペンで書く

fudepen de kaku

to write with a brush pen

自転車で行きましょう。

Jitensha de ikimashō.

Let's go by bicycle.

Adverb

で • (de)

(informal) indicating the following sentence is a consequence of something previously stated: and then; therefore

で？

De?

So what?

で？どうする？

De? Dō suru?

Well? What will you do?

で、オリンピックやめませんか？

De, Orinpikku yamemasen ka?

Then, should we just give up the Olympic Games?

Verb

で • (de)

is/am/are ... and: the て-form or conjunctive form of だ (da)

これは本で、とても面白いです。

Kore wa hon de, totemo omoshiroi desu.

This is a book, and it is very interesting.

Etymology 3

Assimilated form of て (-te, conjunctive ending). The voicing occurs in the two following euphonic (onbin) environments:

After a nasal sound /N/: For 五段活用 (godan katsuyō, “quintigrade conjugation”) verbs ending in む (-mu), ぬ (-nu), and ぶ (-bu), historical conjunctive forms みて (-mite), にて (-nite), and びて (-bite) underwent hatsuonbin (change of a syllable to /N/) and voicing assimilation, becoming んで (-nde).

読み (yomi, 連用形 (ren'yōkei, “stem or continuative form”) of the verb 読む (yomu)) + て (-te) → 読みて (yomite) → *yomte → modern 読んで (yonde)

/jomi/ + /-te/ → /jomite/ → */jomte/ → /jonde/

After a syllable that previously contained /ᵑɡ/ (modern /ɡ/): For 五段活用 (godan katsuyō) verbs ending in ぐ (-gu), historical conjunctive form ぎて (-gite) underwent nasal I-onbin (change of a syllable to /ĩ/; nasal vowels were lost in Japanese, so this becomes /i/) and voicing assimilation, becoming いで (-ide).

泳ぎ (oyogi, 連用形 (ren'yōkei, “stem or continuative form”) of the verb 泳ぐ (oyogu)) + て (-te) → 泳ぎて (oyogite) → *oyoĩte → modern 泳いで (oyoide)

/ojoᵑɡi/ + /-te/ → /ojoᵑɡite/ → */ojoĩte/ → /ojoide/

See also Japanese conjugation (ren'yōkei base) § Gerund.

Suffix

で • (-de)

voiced form of て (-te) used after 五段活用 (godan katsuyō) verbs ending in む (-mu), ぬ (-nu), ぶ (-bu), and ぐ (-gu): forms the conjunctive form

読む (yomu, “to read”) + で (-de) → 読んで (yonde, “reading”)

死ぬ (shinu, “to die”) + で (-de) → 死んで (shinde, “dying”)

遊ぶ (asobu, “to have fun”) + で (-de) → 遊んで (asonde, “playing”)

泳ぐ (oyogu, “to swim”) + で (-de) → 泳いで (oyoide, “swimming”)

Etymology 4

Considered to be a contraction of にて (nite), where the ni is the continuative form of the negative auxiliary ず (zu). In use since Early Middle Japanese, and first attested in The Tale of the Bamboo Cutter of the late 800s.

Suffix

で • (-de)

[from late 800s] (Classical Japanese, archaic) Attaches to the 未然形 (mizenkei, “irrealis or incomplete form”) of verbs to form the negative conjunctive, equivalent to the modern ないで (-naide) or ずに (-zuni).

Etymology 5

Corrupted from ぜ (ze, sentence-ending particle signifying emphasis).

Particle

で • (de)

(dialectal, chiefly Kansai) sentence-ending particle signifying emphasis

そないな事したらあかんで。

Sonai na koto shitara akan de.

You shouldn't do things like that.
