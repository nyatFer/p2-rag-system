# 見る (Japanese, auxiliary verb, Wiktionary)

見る (Japanese, auxiliary verb, Wiktionary)

Etymology

From Old Japanese. First attested in the Kojiki of 712 CE. From Proto-Japonic *miru. Cognate with 目 (me, “eye”).

Verb

見る • (miru) transitive ichidan (stem 見 (mi), past 見た (mita))

to see, to watch, to observe, to look at something

to look over, to examine, to judge

to take care of, to look after, to keep an eye on somebody

to view something, like paintings, flowers or a movie

映画を見に行く ― eiga o mi ni iku ― to go see a movie

あの監督の映画を見たことはありません。

Ano kantoku no eiga o mita koto wa arimasen.

I haven't seen any of that director's movies before.

みる: (auxiliary, after the te form of a verb)

to try to do (literally, “[do the preceding verb and] see”)

納豆を食べてみませんか。

Nattō o tabete mimasen ka.

Why not try [to eat] natto?

死ぬまで君と生きてみたいよ

shinu made kimi to ikite mitai yo

Until death I want to try to live with you

怪しい扉を開けてみたくないよ

ayashī tobira o akete mitakunai yo

I don't want to try to open the weird door!

(in a conditional phrase) now that; used to express the realisation of new information

言われてみるとそうですよね。

Iwarete miru to sō desu yo ne.

Now that you mention it, you're right.

Usage notes

(sense 5.1): Implies that the action has never been attempted by the subject.
