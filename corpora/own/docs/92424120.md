# あげる (Japanese, auxiliary verb, Wiktionary)

あげる (Japanese, auxiliary verb, Wiktionary)

Etymology

From Old Japanese 上ぐ (agu), following 下二段活用 (shimo nidan katsuyō, “lower bigrade conjugation”), from Proto-Japonic *ank-a-.

Verb

あげる • (ageru) transitive ichidan (stem あげ (age), past あげた (ageta))

上げる, 挙げる: to raise, to lift (to move (something) upwards)

Antonym: 下げる (sageru)

手を上げる ― te o ageru ― to raise one's hand

上げる: to point upwards; to lift (one's face, etc.)

Antonym: 伏せる (fuseru)

顔を上げる ― kao o ageru ― to look up (literally, “to lift one's face”)

上げる: to raise; to increase (something quantifiable)

Antonyms: 下げる (sageru), 落とす (otosu)

音量を上げる ― onryō o ageru ― to raise the volume

上げる, 揚げる: to utter, to cry, to produce, to raise (a voice)

悲鳴を上げる ― himei o ageru ― to shriek; to let out a scream

声を上げて泣く ― koe o agete naku ― to sob

上げる: to vomit, to throw up

Synonym: 吐く (haku)

上げる: (Internet) to upload

Synonym: アップロードする (appurōdo suru)

挙げる: to give (an example)

例を挙げてみれば […] ― Rei o agetemireba […] ― Giving an example […]

挙げる: to put all of one's efforts into

挙げる: to nominate

挙げる: to hold (a ceremony)

結婚式を挙げる ― kekkonshiki o ageru ― to hold a wedding ceremony

揚げる: (cooking) to deep-fry

to offer up (to a god, the Buddha, etc.); to make a offering of

Synonyms: 捧げる (sasageru), 供える (sonaeru)

仏壇に線香をあげる ― butsudan ni senkō o ageru ― to offer up incense at a Buddhist altar

上げる: (chiefly in kana) to give

犬に毎日えさをあげてください。

Inu ni mainichi esa o agete kudasai.

Please give the dog food every day.

上げる: (auxiliary, chiefly in kana) (for the speaker) to do (something) for someone else [with (conjunctive +) て]

本を読んであげる。 ― Hon o yonde ageru. ― I'll read you a book.

お菓子を買ってあげる。 ― O-kashi o katte ageru. ― I'll buy you some sweets.

Usage notes

Wikibooks has more about this subject:
Japanese/Lessons/Giving and Receiving

Japanese has an extensive vocabulary for giving and receiving. While 上げる is used for giving something to someone of equal or lower social standing, 差し上げる (sashiageru) is used for giving to someone of higher rank and やる (yaru) for giving to a non-human or to someone of low rank. It is fairly common to use 上げる for giving something to a pet, especially in casual speech, but some speakers consider this usage nonstandard or incorrect.
