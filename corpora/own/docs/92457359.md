# む (Japanese, suffix, Wiktionary)

む (Japanese, suffix, Wiktionary)

Etymology 1

Derived in the Heian period from writing the man'yōgana kanji 武 in the cursive sōsho style.

Syllable

む • (mu)

The hiragana syllable む (mu). Its equivalent in katakana is ム (mu). It is the thirty-third syllable in the gojūon order; its position is ま行う段 (ma-gyō u-dan, “row ma, section u”).

Etymology 2

Onomatopoeia.

Interjection

む • (mu)

A mumbling sound.

Etymology 3

From Old Japanese. First cited in the Kojiki of 712.

The pronunciation shifted from /mu/ to just nasal /m/ and then to the generalized nasal /ɴ/ by the mid-Heian period (794–1185), leading some writers to use ん (n) instead to write this suffix. The kana ん (n) itself evolved out of a hentaigana (alternative kana form) for む (mu) based on the cursive for kanji 无 (mu). In Classical Japanese texts, the practice now is to pronounce final む (-mu) as ん (-n) instead.

As a separate phonological shift, /mu/ retained the vowel but lost the consonant, becoming a nasalized /ũ/ and then plain /u/. This then often fused with the preceding vowel, ultimately leading to the development of the modern volitional / suppositional /oː/ ending in modern Japanese. See よう#Japanese:_suffix for more detail.

Variously described as suppositional ("seems like"), volitional ("I will"), or hortative ("let's). Ultimately, all of these senses arise from an apparent base meaning of "seem, appear, look like". May be cognate with 目 (me, “eye”), 見る (miru, “to see; to look at”), びる (-biru, “to look like, to seem like, to behave like”).

Suffix

む • (-mu) yodan

(Classical Japanese, after mizenkei) used to form the suppositional / volitional / hortative form of verbs, equivalent to modern う (-u > -ō) / よう (-yō); also used as a mild imperative

Etymology 4

From Old Japanese. Related to ぶ and its descendant びる; see there for more information.

Suffix

む • (-mu) godan (stem み (-mi), past んだ (-nda))

(unproductive) forms verbs from adjective stems, denoting becoming (the state of an adjective), to act (in a certain manner)

痛い (itai, “painful”) → 痛む (itamu, “to hurt, feel painful”)

楽しい (tanoshii, “fun, enjoyable”) → 楽しむ (tanoshimu, “to have fun, enjoy”)

Etymology 5

Native Japanese kun’yomi pronunciation of various Chinese characters.

Etymology 6

Middle Chinese-derived on’yomi pronunciation of various Chinese characters.
