# へん (Japanese, suffix, Wiktionary)

へん (Japanese, suffix, Wiktionary)

Etymology 1

From Classical Japanese せん (sen), from はせん (wa sen), with は merged with the preceding syllable or becoming や, giving やへん (yahen). In turn, はせん is a composition of は (wa, topic marker or emphasis marker), せ (se, “to do”, irrealis stem) and ん (n, “not”).

Similar lenition /s/ → [h] is also seen in other words in Kansai, e.g. さん (san) to Kansai はん (han).

Suffix

へん • (-hen)

(Kansai) marks negation

Synonym: (standard) ない (nai)

そんなことでけへん。 ― Sonna koto dekehen. ― I can't do something like that.

Usage notes

This word is morphologically an inflectional suffix. It is classified as 助動詞 (jodōshi, “auxiliary verb”) in traditional Japanese grammar.

In Kyōto, may change to ひん (hin) in certain phonological environments.

The past tense is へんかった (henkatta).

May affect the vowel at the end of the stem, as in the example above (できる (dekiru) → でけへん (dekehen)).
