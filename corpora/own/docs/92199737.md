# 来る (Japanese, auxiliary verb, Wiktionary)

来る (Japanese, auxiliary verb, Wiktionary)

Etymology 1

From Old Japanese 來 (ku), from Proto-Japonic *ku. Compare the development of modern する (suru) from Old Japanese す.

First attested in the Kojiki of 712.

Cognate with Kunigami 来ん (sun), Miyako 来 (ksï), Okinawan 来ーん (chuun), Yaeyama 来ん (kïn), Yonaguni 来ん (kun).

Verb

来る • (kuru) intransitive kuru (stem 来 (ki), past 来た (kita))

to come (toward the speaker) [from 712]

お客さんがあまり来ないお店

o-kyaku-san ga amari konai o-mise

a shop that does not see many visitors

(of a time) to come; to be upon one

春よ来い。 ― Haru yo koi. ― Come, O spring.

(of a sensation) to come and stimulate [from 1908]

心に来る悲しさ

kokoro ni kuru kanashisa

sadness that strikes the heart

喉に来る辛さ

nodo ni kuru karasa

spiciness that assaults the throat

胃に来る

i ni kuru

to noticeably affect the stomach

メンタルにくる ― mentaru ni kuru ― mentally taxing

(と来たら (to kitara)) when it comes to

(auxiliary) [with (verb in ren'yōkei +) て (te)]

to do something in the direction of the speaker

帰ってきて！ ― Kaette kite! ― Come back!

to go somewhere else, do something, and then come back

パンを買ってくる。 ― Pan o katte kuru. ― I'll go and buy some bread (and come back).

行ってきます。 ― Itte kimasu. ― I'm going out. (literally, “I'll go and come back.”)

to do something and thereby come about, appear, come to be

昔の本が出てきた。 ― Mukashi no hon ga dete kita. ― An old book appeared.

used to express that a certain action having continued or repeated has led to the current state

ずっと耐えてきた。

Zutto taete kita.

I've endured all this time (and thus exist as I am now).

used to express that a state has been reached

寒くなってきた。 ― Samuku natte kita. ― It's got cold.

人数が増えてきた。 ― Ninzū ga fuete kita. ― The number of people has increased.

Usage notes

(etymology 1, verb sense 5): This term is often spelled in kana.

Etymology 2

From 来 (ki, continuative form of etymology 1) + 至る (itaru).

Verb

来る • (kitaru) intransitive godan (stem 来り (kitari), past 来った (kitatta))

to come, to arrive
