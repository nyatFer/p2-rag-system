# い (Japanese, particle, Wiktionary)

い (Japanese, particle, Wiktionary)

Etymology 1

Derived in the Heian period from writing the man'yōgana kanji 以 in the cursive sōsho style.

Syllable

い • (i)

The hiragana syllable い (i). Its equivalent in katakana is イ (i). It is the second syllable in the gojūon order; its position is あ行い段 (a-gyō i-dan, “row a, section i”).

Etymology 2

Corruption of emphatic particle よ (yo) or exclamatory particle や (ya).

Particle

い • (i)

Used in compounds with certain sentence-ending particles to create a familiar effect.

だ (da) → だい (dai)

か (ka) → かい (kai)

Used in compounds with certain sentence-ending particles to create an exclamatory effect.

わ (wa) → わい (wai)

ぞ (zo) → ぞい (zoi)

や (ya) → やい (yai)

Used after verbs to create an exclamatory effect.

勝手にしろい！

Katte ni shiroi!

Have it your way!

Etymology 3

From Old Japanese. Possibly cognate with Korean nominal particle 이 (i).

Used in Old Japanese, still found in use in Chinese works annotated for Japanese readers of Early Middle Japanese in the early Heian period, then becoming more limited and appearing only in specialized Buddhist texts. Fallen into disuse by the late Heian period from the time of so-called “cloistered rule”.

Obsolete. Not used in modern Japanese.

Particle

い • (i)

(obsolete) emphatic subject marker, added to nouns and verb stems

(obsolete) emphatic phrase-final particle, emphasizing preceding phrase

Etymology 4

An extension of use of the -i ending on adjectives. See 形容詞 (keiyōshi, “adjective”) for more details on the original adjective formations.

Suffix

い • (-i) -i (adverbial く (-ku))

(informal) suffix forming adjectives: -ic; -al

チャラチャラ (charachara, “onomatopoeia for jingling”) → チャラい (charai, “flashy”)
