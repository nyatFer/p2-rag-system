# そう (Japanese, suffix, Wiktionary)

そう (Japanese, suffix, Wiktionary)

Etymology 1

/sau/ → /sɔː/ → /soː/

Adverb

そう • (sō)

so, like that, in such a way, such

それはそう。／そりゃそうだろ。

Sore wa sō. ／ Sorya sō daro.

Well, of course.

私もそう思う。

Watashi mo sō omou.

I also think so.

(chiefly in the negative) (not) really; (not) very; (not) that (much)

Synonyms: それほど (sore hodo), 余り (amari)

そう多くない。 ― Sō ōku nai. ― There aren't that many.

そう大したものではない。 ― Sō taishita mono de wa nai. ― It's not really a big deal.

Interjection

そう • (sō)

yes, yeah; it's like that; that's how it is; I see. (interjection to show that the speaker is paying attention)

Etymology 2

/sau/ → /sɔː/ → /soː/

Probably either a shift from 様 (sama) or from 相 (sō). Appears from the Muromachi period.

Possibly influenced by, or developed by analogy to, the adverbial and interjectional 然う (sō) above.

Suffix

そう • (-sō) -na (adnominal そうな (-sō na), adverbial そうに (-sō ni))

seem, seem like, appear

(after the plain form of a verb or adjective) apparently, heard that (relating something heard from someone or somewhere else)

Usage notes

This productive suffix meaning “seeming like” can come after a full phrase in plain form, or after a verb or adjective stem, thereby forming a -na adjective.

After a full phrase in plain form, the resulting phrase implies reported speech, something heard from someone else. For example, the verb 降る (furu, “to fall from the sky”) plus this suffix forms 降るそう (furu sō, “I've heard that it will rain” or “someone told me that it will rain”). The adjective 美味しい (oishī, “delicious”) plus this suffix forms 美味しいそう (oishī sō, “I've heard that it's delicious”).

After a verb or adjective stem, the resulting word means “it looks like X”. For example, the stem 降り (furi-) of the verb 降る (furu, “to fall from the sky”) plus this suffix forms 降りそう (furisō, “it looks like it will rain”). The stem 美味し (oishi-) of the adjective 美味しい (oishī, “delicious”) plus this suffix forms 美味しそう (oishisō, “looks delicious”). However, 良い (ī) + そう (-sō) becomes よさそう (yosasō) and ない (nai, adjective) + そう (-sō) becomes なさそう (nasasō).

The suffix should not be used after stem forms describing visually obvious physical traits like color. Although 赤いそう (akai sō, “I've heard that it's red”) would be valid, *赤そう (*akasō, “it seems red”) would not. Another example of this is that 可愛い (kawaī) means someone looks cute, so it does not need another そう (-sō). (かわいそう (kawaisō) does exist, but means “pitiful” or “poor”.) The suffix should, however, be used when describing another person's mood or mental state. For example, if another person looks glad, one says 嬉しそう (ureshisō, “you seem glad”), which is more natural and polite than saying 嬉しい (ureshī, “you are glad”).

This word is morphologically a clitic after a phrase in plain form, and an inflectional suffix after a verb or adjective stem. It is classified as a 助動詞 (jodōshi, “auxiliary verb”) in traditional Japanese grammar.
